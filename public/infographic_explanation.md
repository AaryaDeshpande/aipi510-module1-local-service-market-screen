# Where should founders interview first?

![Scatterplot of 12 selected U.S. local-service industries. The horizontal axis is the 2023 Census share of employer establishments with fewer than 20 employees; the vertical axis is a directional office/admin wage-payroll proxy combining 2023 Census and May 2025 BLS data; bubble area shows the count of small employer locations. Accounting/tax services, dental offices, legal services, and chiropractors are highlighted as interview starting points rather than proven product opportunities.](market_screen_infographic.png)

This infographic compares 12 deliberately selected U.S. local-service industries to help decide where customer discovery might be most informative—not which software product to build. Bubble size shows the number of employer establishments with fewer than 20 employees in the [2023 Census County Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html); horizontal position shows those locations' share of all employer establishments in the industry. The vertical axis is a **directional estimate**, constructed from average employees per establishment in Census data and the office/administrative employment share and annual mean wage in the [May 2025 BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oessrci.htm).

The most useful comparison is not a single “winner.” Accounting/tax services, dental offices, and legal services each have more than 125,000 small employer locations, while chiropractors have the highest small-location share (99.2%) and office/admin employment share (40.3%) of the selected industries. Veterinary services have a high wage-payroll proxy but fewer small locations (27,497) than those first three. These contrasts make those industries different *interview questions*, not ranked commercial opportunities.

The estimate is **not observed payroll** for a representative small business: Census and BLS use different years and methods, and the estimate applies an industry-wide occupational mix to the average location size across *all* establishments. Census counts locations rather than independent firms and excludes nonemployers. The selected 12 industries are not the full economy. Administrative work is not synonymous with waste or automatable work; the data do not measure a customer's pain, buying authority, existing software, or willingness to pay. The next step is to interview owners, staff, and customers to learn what actually needs improvement.
